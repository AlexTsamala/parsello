## The two content layers

The site keeps these strictly separate, in code and in the UI:

| Layer | Where it lives | Who supplies it |
|---|---|---|
| Researched facts about a destination | `src/content/shipping-rules.ts`, `facts[]` in `src/content/countries.ts` | Research, with a source URL on every item |
| What Parcello does / offers / charges | `src/content/business.ts`, `parcello` field per country | The business only — never researched or inferred |

A researched fact describes the world. A Parcello claim describes the business. They must never be blended into one sentence.

## Deliberately not published

- **Whether the €3 duty applies to private gifts.** The Commission and Council materials frame it around e-commerce and do not state the treatment of gift consignments. The site says the rule mainly concerns e-commerce and does not claim gifts are exempt.
- **Whether the meat/dairy ban applies identically to postal parcels.** The Your Europe page is written for travellers. The site states the prohibition on bringing these goods into the EU and directs readers to the destination's customs authority rather than asserting postal specifics.
- **Country-level delivery times and prohibited-item lists.** Business information — awaiting Parcello. (Per-kg prices are business information too: Parcello supplied them on 2026-10-08, and they live in `business.ts`, never here.)
- **The size of the Georgian community in the Netherlands.** The Germany and France pages carry a diaspora figure because each has one current official source. For the Netherlands the available figures disagree and are stale (a ~2,000 census count from 2006 against 544 Georgian citizens in 2016, measuring different things), so the Dutch page states no number rather than picking one.
- **Finnish Customs pages directly.** tulli.fi blocks automated access, so the recipient-clearance rule is cited from Posti, Finland's national postal operator, and phrased as Posti's explanation.
- **The size of the Georgian community in Finland or Sweden.** No current official figure was found; neither page states a number.
- **Sweden's rules for multi-item gifts above SEK 600.** Tullverket lets the items worth up to SEK 600 stay exempt, which differs from the general EU wording on the FAQ page ("VAT on the whole consignment"). The Swedish page states only the SEK 600 threshold rather than publish two rules that look contradictory.
- **Portugal's VAT from Tax Foundation.** Its table gives only the mainland 23%, so the Portuguese page cites gov.pt, which also lists the Madeira and Azores rates.
- **CTT's clearance rules as universal Portuguese customs law.** The NIF-or-passport requirement is CTT's portal procedure; a parcel arriving through another carrier may be cleared differently. The page always attributes it to CTT, and states no CTT service fee.
- **The size of the Georgian community in Portugal.** No figure was researched; the Portuguese page states no number.
- **Baltic postal fees, duty rates and dates.** Omniva quotes a service-fee amount and a flat duty rate for gifts between €45 and €700, and Lietuvos paštas a declaration fee; the pages say a fee exists but state no amount or rate, because third-party prices change. Omniva's page also carries a "1 July 2026" declaration date that automated summaries rendered inconsistently, so no date from it is published. The Estonian page names no Estonian embassy in Georgia: the MFA page does not give one.
- **The size of the Georgian community in Lithuania, Latvia or Estonia.** No figure was researched; none of the three pages states a number.
- **Austrian and Belgian postal fees.** The BMF page says Österreichische Post charges a Zollstellungsentgelt and bpost lists customs-formalities fees by value band; the pages say a fee exists but state no amount, because third-party prices change. bpost's lowest band also failed to render on its page (a template placeholder), so no lower threshold is published.
- **Postal procedures as universal customs law.** The Austrian declaration rule describes Österreichische Post and the Belgian rules describe bpost; a parcel arriving through another carrier may be cleared differently. Both pages always attribute the rule to the post, as the Portuguese page does with CTT.
- **Belgium's bilingual Brussels and dual-language place names.** True, but only third-party sources were reachable (belgium.be serves a captcha), so the page states the three official languages and nothing about how addresses are written.
- **An embassy of Belgium in Georgia.** The MFA page gives Georgia's mission in Belgium but not Belgium's in Georgia, so the page names none.
- **The size of the Georgian community in Austria or Belgium.** No figure was researched; neither page states a number.
- **Romania's 25-lei fee on non-EU parcels.** In force from 1 Jan 2026 on items under €150, but the Finance Ministry frames it around sellers and platforms, no official source says whether it applies to a gift between private individuals, and press reports say it is due to be withdrawn once the EU handling fee arrives (by November 2026). Too unsettled to publish.
- **Poșta Română's VAT figure and fees.** Its help page still describes VAT at 19%, so the rate is cited from Tax Foundation (21% since August 2025) and the post's page only for its procedures. Its customs presentation and brokerage fees (12.20 and 22.37 lei) are not published, because third-party prices change. The page's exchange-rate sentence ("first working day of October, applied from 1 January") does not say what it converts, so it is not used.
- **Romania's euro target date.** The European Commission page still gives 2024 as Romania's target year, which has passed; the page says only that Romania is not in the euro area and the leu is not in ERM II.
- **An embassy of Romania in Georgia, or when Georgia's embassy in Bucharest opened.** The MFA page gives neither, so the page names only Georgia's embassy in Bucharest.
- **Postal procedures as universal customs law (Romania).** The declaration and gift-marking rules are Poșta Română's; the page always attributes them to the post.
- **An Post's administration fee and the coming EU handling fee (Ireland).** An Post changed its fee on 3 Feb 2026 and press reports describe a further ~€2 EU handling fee from November 2026; the page says a fee may apply but states no amount. The Irish page likewise attributes the payment procedure to An Post, as other carriers may differ.
- **Denmark's DKK 360 versus the EU's €45.** Toldstyrelsen states the gift threshold as DKK 360, which is more than €45 at the krone's central rate (about DKK 336). The page states the Danish figure, attributed to the Agency, next to the EU-wide €45 rule, as the Swedish page does with SEK 600, and does not convert either. Toldstyrelsen's rule for consignments of several items is also left out, for the same reason as Sweden's.
- **PostNord's payment procedure (Denmark).** postnord.dk refuses scripted requests (403), so nothing from it is published.
- **The size of the Georgian community in Ireland or Denmark.** No figure was researched; neither page states a number.

- **Northern Ireland's rules.** GOV.UK charges duty and a per-item handling fee on goods "at risk" of entering the EU; the page covers Great Britain only and says just that GOV.UK publishes separate rules for Northern Ireland. Whether Parcello delivers there is open question #24.

The Italian page always attributes the procedure to Poste Italiane, as another carrier may clear a parcel differently.
- **The lira exchange deadline.** The European Commission gives 29 Feb 2012, but Italy ended the exchange early in December 2011 and that decision was later challenged in court; too tangled to state, so the page gives only the 2002 changeover.
- **Remittances from Italy, and the size of the Georgian community there.** Italy is reported as the largest EU source of money transfers to Georgia, but the figures found were press reports of National Bank of Georgia releases, not the releases themselves, so no figure is published. No official community figure was researched.
- **Livigno and Campione d'Italia.** Both sit outside the EU VAT area, but sources disagree on Livigno's customs status, so the page does not mention either.
- **The size of the Georgian community in Croatia, or Croatia's mission to Georgia.** The MFA page gives no community figure; its only mention of Croatia's ambassador (resident in Baku) is a June 2023 news item, too old to publish as current.

The Tbilisi–Amsterdam schedules only turned up on commercial flight aggregators, which change without notice — not a citable source.
